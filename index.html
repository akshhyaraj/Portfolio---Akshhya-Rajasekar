CASE FILE — GOVERNANCE & FINANCIAL RESTRUCTURING

WeWork

Group project, 3 members — I led the financial and governance analysis behind the recommendations

$47.2BFuture lease obligations
$219K/hrCash burn rate
2–3 moCash runway at collapse

This case asked us to diagnose why WeWork nearly collapsed in 2019 and recommend what its new leadership should do about it. The interesting part, given my background in corporate governance, was that the financial distress and the governance failure were the same problem viewed from two angles — a board with no real oversight over leases, capital allocation, and related-party deals (the founder's family controlled a real estate fund with zero board review) let expansion outrun cash generation until the company was burning $700M a quarter with two to three months of runway left.

We built the financial case for what came next: separating the chairman and CEO roles, reversing the founder's succession clause, and — on the numbers side — a set of concrete stabilization moves, including raising administrative fees from 8% to 10% and cutting non-essential spend, projected to add roughly $66M in annual revenue against $900M in first-half-2019 losses. We also built a formal expansion approval framework requiring board sign-off on market screening, financial analysis, and risk assessment before any future lease commitment, directly addressing the governance gap that let the crisis happen in the first place.

What made this project useful to me specifically was seeing how cleanly a governance failure translates into a financial one — a lesson that applies well beyond WeWork.

CASE FILE — CAPITAL INVESTMENT, FINANCING & M&A

Burton Sensors, Inc.

Group project, 4 members — two-part case; I led the DCF, NPV/IRR, and M&A valuation work and the financing recommendations

$790,925NPV, thermowell project
22.3%IRR
8.21%WACC
3.9 yrsPayback

Burton Sensors was profitable but structurally cash-constrained: rapid sales growth of 19–24% had outpaced internal cash generation, and both of the company's bank covenants were already breached at the end of 2016. The case asked us to recommend a path forward on three fronts at once — the growth strategy, the financing structure, and a proposed capital investment in new thermowell manufacturing equipment.

I built the discounted cash flow model for the thermowell project: a WACC of 8.21% (built from a 6% market risk premium, 3% risk-free rate, and a relevered equity beta of 1.56 from sensor-industry comparables), producing an NPV of $790,925 and an IRR of 22.3% against a $1.25M initial outlay. On paper, the project was clearly value-creating.

The harder call was financing. Both covenants were breached, the bank was tightening rather than extending credit, and the OTC market was too thin for a public raise — leaving a private equity placement as the only real option. I worked through the numbers on the placement's dilution (a 33% increase in share count, EPS declining from $0.38 to $0.29) against the alternative of continuing to breach covenants. My recommendation was to raise the equity immediately to cure the covenants, and defer the thermowell project — despite its strong returns — until 2018–2019, once operating cash flow turned meaningfully positive and the capital outlay could be funded without trading off the company's covenant position.

PART II — ELECTRO-ENGINEERING ACQUISITION

The follow-on case asked us to evaluate an alternative to the equity placement: acquiring Electro-Engineering (EE), a fiber-optic sensor manufacturer, in a stock-for-stock deal. I built the DCF valuation of EE — post-synergy pro forma financials through 2021E, an 8.11% WACC, and comparables from Opsens and CyberOptics — arriving at an equity value of $8,328K ($7.57/share) against the owner's $6,866K asking price, a 17.6% discount to intrinsic value.

I recommended proceeding with the acquisition immediately: because the deal is stock-funded, it requires no cash and resolves both covenants on closing (liabilities/equity falls from 4.43x to 1.06x), while the thermowell project — still value-creating, but requiring $1.25M of cash Burton didn't have — stays deferred to 2019, once the combined company's cash position could fund it without covenant risk. Sequencing mattered more than either decision in isolation.

CASE FILE — MANAGERIAL ECONOMICS & ROUTE ANALYSIS

United Airlines — SFO–Bangalore Route

Group project, 3 members — I led the demand modeling and cost/marginal-analysis work; the regression analysis was led by a teammate

6,000Monthly market passengers
18Recommended monthly flights
$675.3KMarginal cost / round trip

Acting as planning analysts for United Airlines, our team evaluated whether the airline should launch a direct SFO–Bangalore route — one United had previously announced and then cancelled in 2023. The case required building an actual economic model rather than a qualitative recommendation: a demand function, a cost structure, and a marginal analysis to test where the route made sense.

We ran a regression to test whether ticket price significantly affected demand on this route, using Python's linregress to cross-check the results — the p-values came back statistically insignificant, indicating inelastic demand, consistent with a market split between time-sensitive business travelers and visiting-friends-and-relatives travelers with limited scheduling flexibility. From there, we sized the addressable market (6,000 passengers/month, with Air India already serving 2,400), built a total cost function (roughly $2.42M fixed plus $675.3K variable per round trip), and compared marginal revenue against marginal cost across flight frequencies to identify the profit-maximizing schedule — arriving at a recommendation of 18 monthly round trips for United, filling the demand Air India's existing service didn't cover.

CASE FILE — EQUITY VALUATION

Tesla, Inc.

Group project, 4 members ("Team Tesla") — I led the recommendation and valuation methodology

$429.24Market price, Dec 2 2025
$288.90–$302.21Our price target
SELLRating

Our team was asked to issue a rated recommendation on Tesla as of December 2025. I led the recommendation slide and the valuation methodology behind it — deciding how to weight and reconcile the different valuation approaches into a single, defensible view.

We built a blended valuation combining a relative valuation ($308.86/share, based on EV/Revenue comparables weighted toward Tesla's autonomy and compute segments — Nvidia and Alphabet at a 60% weight each, versus 15–25% for GM, Ford, and Enphase) with a discounted cash flow model ($175.79/share). We excluded a separate free cash flow to equity approach ($40.15) as unreliable at Tesla's growth stage. The blended result put fair value meaningfully below the market's $429.24, which is where the SELL rating and our $288.90–$302.21 target came from.

The most useful part of this project wasn't the model itself — it was defending a conclusion the market clearly disagreed with. That meant being explicit about where the gap came from: the market was pricing in autonomy and robotaxi optionality more aggressively than our comparables supported, and we said so directly rather than softening the call to split the difference.

CASE FILE — VENTURE FINANCIAL MODEL

AuditPulse

Group project, 5 members ("Team Manatee") — I served as CFO & Strategy Lead, building the financial model and fundraising materials

900KUS SMBs in TAM
15,600Bay Area SAM
$12.8MYear 5 revenue (modeled)

AuditPulse was a hypothetical AI-powered compliance and audit-readiness platform for small and mid-sized businesses — the kind of company that would otherwise pay for manual compliance monitoring (multi-state nexus, 1099 reporting, related-party detection, GAAP checks) as it scaled toward an audit or a funding round.

My role was building the full financial model behind the venture: a five-year projection with tiered SaaS pricing ($51–$206/month depending on tier), unit economics that improved from a negative LTV in Year 2 (customer acquisition cost of $492 against early churn) to a $150 LTV by Year 5 as CAC fell to $216, and a bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM build starting from 900,000 U.S. small businesses that need audit-grade financials, narrowing to a 15,600-company Bay Area entry market. I used that model to build the company's Series Seed fundraising case, translating a five-phase product roadmap (from a manual prototype to a full multi-entity, auditor-collaboration platform) into the milestones an investor would actually want to see funded.

This project was less about a single number and more about connecting a product roadmap to a financing plan — showing which capabilities needed to exist before the next round of funding made sense, not just what the business might eventually look like at scale.
